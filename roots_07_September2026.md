***What we've been reading***

# The importance of stupidity in scientific research

This essay is almost 20 years old, but it became my favourite when I started my journey as a researcher, and I never miss the opportunity to share it around :)  
Whether you're a scientist yourself, or you realised that research is not for you but you still love science and training, this is essential reading in my opinion:  
First, the author summarises beautifully the need to feel stupid in order to do good research. This is what I normally share as the need for humbleness to do sustainable and human-centered science. If you always think you know what you're doing, it is most likely you're doing it wrong.  
Second, it highlights the need to teach students how to feel stupid, in a productive way (as opposed to self deprecating). I think **being ok with feeling stupid is essential** to navigate the uncertainty that is inherent to research and innovation, and that is one of the reasons why a lot of people don't enjoy research after a while... navigating the frustration of trying and not succeeding 90% of the times is a skill that is barely taught (at least in the western world), and that would help researchers, but really, any human to feel happier while enjoying their work.

> *Productive stupidity means being ignorant by choice.*

This sums up my love for this essay: Once you are ignorant by choice, you open yourself to discover anything and everything. **And... well, what can be more beautiful than that?**

[Read it here](https://web.stanford.edu/~fukamit/schwartz-2008.pdf)
